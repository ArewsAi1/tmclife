Why Homeowners Choose Trex

Trex decking combines the appearance of wood with lower routine maintenance than traditional lumber. The composite material resists common problems such as rot, splintering and insect damage, making it a practical choice for homeowners who want to spend less time sanding, staining and sealing.

Trex offers color families ranging from warm brown and red tones to contemporary grays. Board color should be considered alongside the home’s siding, masonry, roof, landscaping and the amount of direct sun the deck receives.

A successful installation also depends on framing, ventilation, drainage, fastening, fascia and stair details. Ogden Decks helps coordinate the complete system rather than selecting the deck board in isolation.

Trex Decking and Utah Weather

Northern Utah decks experience intense summer sun, snow loads, freeze-thaw cycles and large temperature changes. Composite decking performs well when the framing, board spacing, fastening and drainage follow product requirements.

Darker colors may feel warmer in direct sun, while shaded areas can hold snow and moisture longer. We evaluate orientation, airflow and how the deck connects to the home before recommending details.

Trex Deck Maintenance

Trex does not require traditional staining or sealing, but it still benefits from routine cleaning. Leaves, dirt and organic debris should be removed, especially where moisture can remain trapped. Snow should be cleared with tools and methods that will not damage the surface.

Fasteners, fascia, railing and stairs should be checked periodically. If a board becomes damaged, replacement may be possible, though matching depends on the age, product line and sun exposure of the existing deck.

Trex Repair and Resurfacing

An existing wood deck may be suitable for Trex resurfacing if the framing is structurally sound, properly spaced and capable of supporting the new system. We inspect joists, beams, posts, ledger connections and stairs before recommending reuse.

For existing composite decks, we provide board replacement, fascia repairs, railing work, stair correction and structural repairs. When an exact color match is unavailable, a coordinated border or larger resurfacing area may produce a better visual result.

Trex Versus Wood Decking

Trex Composite

Lower recurring maintenance, resistance to splinters and rot, consistent board profiles and a broad selection of coordinated components.

Traditional Wood

Natural appearance, familiar installation methods and the ability to change stain color, with ongoing cleaning, sealing and repair requirements.

The right choice depends on budget, appearance, sun exposure, maintenance expectations and how long the homeowner plans to use the space.
