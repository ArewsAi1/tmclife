Planning the Right Structure

A pergola should be planned around the way the space is used, the direction of the sun, nearby doors and windows, the roofline, deck framing and the desired level of weather protection.

Attached structures require careful coordination with the home. Freestanding pergolas offer more placement flexibility but still need appropriate footings, clearances and drainage planning.

For deck-mounted pergolas, the support loads should be considered with the deck structure rather than attached as an afterthought. Post locations may affect railing, stairs, furniture and circulation.

Design Details That Matter

Orientation determines how much shade the structure provides at different times of day. Slat direction, spacing, height and roofline relationship all influence comfort and appearance.

Lighting, fans, privacy screens and climbing plants should be discussed before construction because they may affect blocking, electrical preparation and finish details.

In Utah, snow exposure, wind, drainage and freeze-thaw conditions should be considered along with appearance.

Integrating a Pergola with a Deck

The most successful projects treat the deck and pergola as one system. Post placement should align with framing where possible, preserve walking paths and avoid conflicts with railing or stairs.

For an existing deck, the structure should be evaluated before adding pergola loads. Reinforcement or independent footings may be appropriate depending on the design.
