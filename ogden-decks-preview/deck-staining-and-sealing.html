Preparation Determines the Result

New stain cannot correct loose boards, rot, failing fasteners or damaged framing. Before refinishing, the deck should be evaluated for repair needs and for the condition of the existing coating.

Cleaning removes dirt, biological growth and surface contamination. Depending on the prior finish and desired result, the deck may also need stain removal, brightening, sanding or localized board replacement.

Applying finish to wood that is too wet, poorly prepared or incompatible with the existing coating can lead to uneven color, peeling or premature failure.

Choosing a Finish

Transparent and semi-transparent products show more of the wood grain, while more opaque finishes provide stronger color coverage. The right choice depends on the wood species, previous coating, desired appearance and willingness to maintain the surface.

Darker colors may absorb more heat. Highly exposed horizontal surfaces usually weather faster than protected vertical surfaces. Stairs, rail caps and areas near sprinklers may require more frequent attention.

Utah Weather and Maintenance

Sun exposure, snow, freeze-thaw cycles and water movement all influence coating life. Decks with good drainage and airflow generally dry more consistently than areas where water collects or landscaping keeps the wood damp.

There is no universal maintenance schedule. The finish type, exposure, traffic and surface condition determine when cleaning or recoating is appropriate.

When Replacement Makes More Sense

Refinishing improves the surface but does not restore severely deteriorated wood or structural components. Extensive rot, widespread splitting, movement or repeated coating failure may indicate that repair, resurfacing or replacement is the better long-term option.
